LinkedIn Ads  ·  Strategy Proposal

Kubiieo LinkedIn Ads Strategy

A phased approach designed to layer on top of the Google Ads activity already running. The campaigns lead with AWS Cost Optimisation as the primary offer, the quickest way into a conversation with a cloud buyer and the engagement the team is set up to deliver fastest. The first weeks build a Kubiieo audience on LinkedIn around that message. The later weeks turn that audience into leads, and then into booked cost assessments.

Kubiieo's buyers are CTOs, Heads of Cloud, and infrastructure decision-makers at mid-market and enterprise organisations across Australia and New Zealand. They spend their working day on LinkedIn, and they search Google when something breaks or a project lands on their desk. Google Ads already covers the second moment well. The first moment, the long stretch where the buyer is forming opinions and shortlisting partners months before they ever type "AWS managed services" into a search bar, is where LinkedIn earns its place.

On the 8 May call, Baz asked about boosting LinkedIn posts after the Advanced Tier post did well organically. Boosting is fine for pushing content that already works, and that option stays open. The reason this proposal centres on structured campaigns instead is that boosting reaches the audience Kubiieo already has, mostly existing followers and their connections, while a proper campaign reaches the audience Kubiieo wants to have. The cost per click is higher than Meta, generally in the $8 to $15 range for Australian IT decision-makers, but the audience there is the right audience: a single closed engagement in the $80,000 to $150,000 range covers roughly twelve months of LinkedIn ad budget at the recommended starting tier.

One decision shapes every campaign below: the lead message is AWS Cost Optimisation. It carries the most demand in the market right now, it opens a conversation with a cloud buyer faster than any other angle, and it is the engagement Kubiieo delivers fastest because the tooling and expertise already sit in-house. The AWS Cost Optimisation creative produced for the Google Ads channel, the assessment offer and the landing-page copy, moves straight across to LinkedIn, so the channel launches with material that already works.

Two more angles run alongside cost optimisation at a smaller share of impressions, so Phase 1 reads which message the audience moves on before any lead-gen budget goes out. Security is the natural follow-on for the same FinOps and platform leaders. Onshore vs offshore speaks to Australian businesses stuck with an offshore vendor that delivers little value and is hard to walk away from. TPG Telecom is the live proof point for this pain right now. The creative names the pattern without naming specific offshore vendors, and the angle is built on the sourcing-reality-check material Baz shared on 23 May.

02

Three phases, three jobs

How the funnel is built

The plan splits the work into three phases that hand off to each other. Phase 1 builds the Kubiieo audience on LinkedIn and gathers data on which creative angles land. Phase 2 turns those engaged people into named leads through a downloadable asset. Phase 3 puts the strongest converters in front of the new landing pages and Conversation Ads, and that is where the channel becomes pipeline-attributable.

Each phase produces the audience that the next phase relies on. Running them all at once on a brand-new LinkedIn account spreads the algorithm thin: the platform's optimisation needs people who have already shown interest before it can find more of them, and that engagement signal has to be built before it can be retargeted. Direct-response ads launched cold to a cold audience are the most common reason LinkedIn campaigns post a CPL above $200 in the first month.

03

Phase detail and kill rules

The cards above lay out what each phase does. The section below explains how the phases hand off to each other, the unique mechanic each one relies on, and the spending limits that trigger a pause before performance quietly slips.

Phase 1 · Audience build
Start here

Phase 1 runs first because Phases 2 and 3 depend on the audience it produces. Launching lead-gen forms or retargeting against a brand-new LinkedIn audience routinely pushes CPL above $150 on the platform, and Phase 1 is what protects against that failure mode.

The three parallel campaigns feed each other in useful ways. The awareness creative builds brand familiarity, which lifts the conversion rate on the page follower campaign by roughly 15 to 25%. The video views campaign produces the strongest retargeting signal of the three, because the platform records who watched a defined threshold, and that audience is what converts best once Phase 3 retargets it.

Across all three campaigns, AWS Cost Optimisation runs as the lead creative. Security and the onshore-vs-offshore angle run underneath it at smaller shares of impressions. Phase 1 is where the three-way question gets answered cheaply: the engagement data shows which message the audience moves on before any lead-gen budget goes out, so the lead magnet in Phase 2 is built around the angle that already proved itself.

The handoff into Phase 2 is gated on engaged audience size rather than on calendar weeks. If Phase 1 needs an extra couple of weeks to clear 3,000 engaged people, the rest of the timeline moves back by the same amount.

Phase 1 kill rule

If cost per follower is above $25 and engagement rate is below 1.2% after $1,500 in spend, the targeting is wrong. We pause, audit the audience definition together, and rebuild before topping up. We will not move to Phase 2 until the engaged audience clears 3,000 unique people.

Phase 2 · Lead capture
Overlaps Phase 1

Phase 2 launches around week 4, while Phase 1 is still running at reduced budget. It uses the engaged audience that Phase 1 produced as the primary targeting layer, which is what keeps cost per lead inside the $50 to $75 band. The same lead-gen creative run cold against a brand-new audience routinely posts CPL above $150 on LinkedIn.

Getting the leads into a place Sean and Baz can act on is the other half of this phase. While Baz's Zoho admin invite is pending, leads route into the new sales@kubiieo.com inbox (which flows through PagerDuty and lands in the #kubiieo-sales Slack channel), with a parallel weekly export to a holding list (a Google Sheet or Airtable view) so nothing is lost in the gap. Once Zoho admin access is in place, the lead-gen form feeds straight into Zoho Leads with the Lead Source tagged per campaign, and the holding list is added into the live flow.

The two follow-on magnets (security checklist and sourcing reality check) are kept ready and only go live if their angle beats the cost creative on Phase 1 engagement rate. Running every magnet at once splits spend across audiences and slows them all down, so the second and third are held back until Phase 1 reads.

Phase 2 kill rule

If CPL on the lead-gen form is above $90 after $2,000 in spend, the magnet is the wrong asset for this audience. Iterate the headline and re-test for two weeks before pausing the campaign.

Phase 3 · Conversion
Gated on Zoho

Phase 3 opens around week 8, once the engaged audience from Phase 1 is big enough to retarget against and the Phase 2 lead list is producing signal. This is the phase where LinkedIn becomes pipeline-attributable.

The attribution mechanic is what makes this phase work. The LinkedIn Insight Tag installed in the existing GTM container fires on every landing-page form submit, and the UTM parameters already wired into the landing-page hidden fields tag each submission back to the campaign and creative that produced it. We read the attribution against Zoho deal stage at the 90-day review.

Conversation Ads are the one piece of Phase 3 that needs Baz's direct involvement. The ad runs from a named sender, defaulting to him, and replies from senior cloud titles land in an inbox of his choosing. The retargeting and lookalike layers run independently.

By week 12 there should be enough booked-consultation and pipeline data to call whether the channel scales up or pulls back to Phase 1 awareness only.

Phase 3 kill rule

If cost per landing-page conversion is above $400 after $3,000 in spend, the bottleneck is the page or the offer, not the ad. The next move is a single-variable test on the landing-page hero, not more ad spend.

Audience targeting recipe

LinkedIn rewards an audience that is tightly defined enough to keep the wrong people from spending Kubiieo's budget, but large enough for the algorithm to optimise inside. The combination below sits at roughly 60,000 to 90,000 active members across Australia and New Zealand. Three ad sets test slices of it against each other.

Because the lead offer is cost optimisation, the targeting leans toward the people who own or answer for the cloud bill: Head of Cloud, FinOps, platform, and infrastructure leaders, plus the engineering managers who get asked to explain a rising AWS invoice. The security and onshore-vs-offshore angles reach the same seniority through the DevSecOps and compliance filters, so all three messages test inside the same audience without standing up separate ones.

05

Budget: test first, scale on signal

The numbers below are options, not a fixed commitment. The recommended path starts with a small test budget, watches whether Phase 1 produces the audience growth and creative signal it is meant to, and only then scales up. Two things should drive the decision to move from one tier to the next: Google Ads producing measurable returns (which gives the business cash flow to invest into LinkedIn), and LinkedIn's own early signal (which tells us whether spending more is worth it). One closed deal in the typical Kubiieo range covers a year of LinkedIn spend at the committed tier, which is what makes the test worth running in the first place.

Tier Monthly spend What it covers When it makes sense
Test $500-$1,000 Phase 1 only, smaller daily caps, two creative variants. First read on CPF, CPM, and which creative angle the audience responds to. Starting point. Validates the targeting before committing to a monthly figure.
Starter $1,500 Phase 1 across all three campaign objectives. Audience build of 2,000-3,500 engaged people. One creative test every two weeks. Once Phase 1 test produces a clean CPF number and the targeting is settled.
Committed Recommended $3,000 Phase 1 plus Phase 2 in parallel from week 4. Lead-gen forms live, audience build of 5,000-8,000 engaged people, expecting 30 to 60 leads/month. Once the engaged audience clears 3,000 and the lead-gen creative is approved.
Scale $5,000+ All three phases concurrently. Retargeting, lookalike layer, Conversation Ads on named accounts. Expecting 6 to 12 booked consultations/month. Once the committed tier produces measurable pipeline and Google Ads is producing the cash flow to fund it.
How the budget actually scales

The plan is built so that each tier upgrade is funded by results, not by faith. The test tier produces the data needed to decide whether the starter tier is worth it. The starter tier produces the audience that makes the committed tier productive. The committed tier produces the pipeline that justifies the scale tier. If Google Ads starts producing closed consultations, a portion of that revenue can be reinvested into LinkedIn, which means the LinkedIn budget grows from real returns rather than from a pure upfront commitment.

Campaign management, creative production, weekly optimisation and reporting sit inside the existing weekly engagement we are already running for Kubiieo. The figures above are media spend paid directly to LinkedIn, with no separate management fee on top.

Reporting cadence

The reporting layer matches what is already running for Google Ads, so Baz reads one consistent format across both channels.

  • Weekly: short Slack post in the Kubiieo channel with spend, results, what changed, and what is being tested next.
  • Every two weeks: a one-page HTML report per channel, in the same brand-consistent template as the existing Google Ads reporting.
  • End of Phase 1 (week 6): phase-end review with Baz and Sean. Decision on whether to open Phase 2 spend.
  • End of 90 days (week 12): attribution review covering pipeline sourced, consultations booked, and deals progressing. Scale-up or pull-back call sits here.

90-day execution timeline

The timeline below is the path if all phases hit their kill rules cleanly. Phase boundaries move back by a week or two if the audience build is slower than expected.

Setup. LinkedIn Campaign Manager configured against the Kubiieo account. Insight Tag installed via the existing GTM container GTM-TV9VXWHD. Conversion events defined for follow, video view, lead-gen submit, and landing-page submit. Opening carousel creative built from the 8 May screenshots.
Week 1-2
Phase 1 launch. Awareness, video views, and page follower campaigns all go live together. Three creative variants per objective. Audience size and pacing monitored daily through the first seven days, daily budget caps in place to prevent overspend during learning.
Week 2-3
First creative cut. Lowest-performing variant paused, two new variants rotated in. AWS Summit recap content added if the team captures usable footage on 13-14 May.
Week 4
Phase 1 mid-checkpoint. Engaged audience size, CPF, and video completion read against the kill rule. Phase 2 lead magnet PDF finalised. Newsletter setup completed inside LinkedIn.
Week 5-6
Phase 2 launch. Lead-gen form goes live with the AWS Cost Reduction Playbook. Two headline variants test against each other. Phase 1 continues at a reduced budget.
Week 6
Phase 1 review with Baz and Sean. Full data read. Decision on whether to continue Phase 2 spend at current pace and on Phase 3 readiness.
Week 7-8
Zoho integration verified. Once Baz's admin invite lands, the lead-gen webhook routes directly into Zoho Leads with Lead Source set per campaign. The manual weekly export bridges the gap until then.
Week 8-9
Phase 3 launch. Retargeting to the three landing pages goes live. Lookalike audience built from TPG Telecom and Satori Group profiles. Conversation Ads launched on a small budget against the named-account list.
Week 10-11
Optimisation pass. Bid adjustments per ad set. Underperforming creative cycled out. If the primary 200-5,000 employee band is running out of fresh people, the audience-expansion test on the 5,000+ band runs here.
Week 12
90-day attribution review. Pipeline sourced, consultations booked, deals progressing. Recommendation to either scale to the next tier or pull back, depending on what the data says.

What we need to launch the test

This is a small test with no monthly commitment attached. Over the first two or three weeks the plan is to:

  • Spend a small amount and get the first real numbers back.
  • See whether Phase 1 grows the audience and shows which creative lands.
  • Use that data to decide whether scaling up makes sense.
The three items needed from Baz

1. Confirm a small test budget cap for the first two to three weeks. $500 to $1,000 is enough to read whether the audience build is working.

2. Confirm the billing setup on LinkedIn Campaign Manager. We do not yet know whether a billing method is attached at the campaign account level. If nothing is set up, Baz drops a card in. If something is already there, no action needed.

3. Approve the opening creative built from the AWS Cost Optimisation material already in use on the Google Ads channel, plus the smaller-share creatives for security and the onshore-vs-offshore angle (the sourcing-reality-check artefact Baz shared on 23 May informs the third). One review cycle, written feedback in Slack is enough.

From there, we handle the rest internally: LinkedIn Campaign Manager configuration, Insight Tag deployment into the existing GTM container, conversion events, audience targeting, creative production, and launch. The first numbers come back within seven to ten days of going live, and the first formal review with Baz lands at week 6.

Inside the team: Antonio leads campaign setup, weekly optimisation, and reporting. Sean covers messaging direction, creative review, and the organic LinkedIn content cadence already running on the page.
