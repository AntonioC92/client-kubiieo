Baz's targeting feedback — 23 June 2026

Baz shared a detailed note on 23 June clarifying the ideal customer profile and the types of companies he wants to reach. The full direction is below, followed by what it changes in the campaign targeting.

Avoid large enterprise. They almost always have a plethora of in-house engineering or consultancy incumbents which are very hard to dethrone. Procurement will ask "Why can't we do this internally, or an existing vendor of ours do it?" Onboarding can take up to 6 months and it is very time consuming. We only got into TPG because Eugene and I used to work there and had contacts.

Ideally we should filter based on enterprise size and go for small to medium — likely small initially — with less red tape and less decision makers and internal politics, as I would like to hit up founders directly and have a much better chance in the smaller enterprise space.

Avoid companies in tech, fintech, banking, unless they are small. Almost always have some engineering capacity internally already. Banks are slow, slow AF, and again, will take ages to onboard.

Industries outside of tech or completely unrelated to tech are more likely to outsource. Myself and the guys have worked with enterprise that deal in entertainment, raw materials, constructions, manufacturing. I would almost say that non-tech companies are better because they have less engineering internally and are more reliant on tech consultancies.

No reason to correlate or map existing clients or case studies to prospects. Public cloud is public cloud no matter what industry you are in. AWS infrastructure is agnostic of their industry. Our guys have worked with clients in almost every industry and honestly it is all the same shit to us.

I want to focus on getting clients outside of the industries we are already dealing with to give us more exposure. Looking for quick wins, quick logos, quick small pieces of work to harvest reviews, testimonials, case studies to beef up our marketing material. Hence I want to start with small enterprise. Definitely think outside the box regarding the type of enterprises.

Baz Lalor, 23 June 2026
04

What this changes in the targeting

Baz's feedback shifts the ICP significantly. The current live targeting is built around cloud and engineering leaders at mid-to-large companies. The revised direction targets founders and small-company decision makers at businesses that run on AWS but have no internal cloud team.

SettingCurrent (Campaign 1)Revised direction
Company size No filter applied (~220k audience) 50–500 employees as the primary band. Less procurement, fewer decision makers, founders reachable directly.
Industries Technology, Financial Services, Telecommunications, Energy, Government Non-tech first: Construction, Manufacturing, Entertainment, Retail, Logistics, Food and Beverage, Professional Services (legal, accounting). Tech stays as secondary only for small companies.
Job titles Head of Cloud, DevOps, SRE, Infrastructure, FinOps Add Founder, Co-Founder, CEO as primary targets for small companies. The Founder at a 100-person construction firm is the person who signed the AWS contract and answers the bill.
Avoid Not explicitly excluded Large enterprise (1,000+ employees as primary), tech companies, fintech, banking. These have internal engineering, slow procurement, or both.
Creative angle Technical — speaks to engineers and cloud leaders Shift to business owner language for the founder-facing ad set: "the bill goes up and nobody internally owns it" rather than technical jargon about overprovisioned capacity.
Recommended approach

Keep Campaign 1 running as-is until the budget runs out on 15 July. In parallel, build a new ad set targeting Founder/CEO/Co-Founder titles at 50–500 employee companies in non-tech industries, using the founder-facing ad copy in Section 05. Run both and compare CPL at the two-week mark. The winning audience determines the Phase 2 build.
