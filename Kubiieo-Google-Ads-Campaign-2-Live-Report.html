Campaign #2 — Assessments

This one took a bit longer to build than Campaign 1 because of the three-funnel structure, but that structure is exactly what makes it worth running. Each ad group speaks to a different pain point. The keywords are tight. The ads reached Good strength before going live.

Campaign 1 was always about people already looking for a managed services partner. Campaign 2 is different. It goes after people in the middle of a specific problem: they want to know why their AWS bill is so high, they're thinking about a migration but can't quantify it, or they've got a security review on their plate. Those are all high-intent searches and they're all pointing at something we can solve with a free assessment.

Why A$15/day

Campaign 1 runs at A$50/day because it covers a broad managed services audience and we had confidence in the conversion rate from day one. Campaign 2 is testing three specific assessment funnels. Starting at A$15/day lets the campaign collect real search term data without burning budget before we know which funnel converts best. Once we see which ad group is pulling qualified leads, we can shift budget toward it.

02 The three ad groups

Each ad group targets a distinct buyer intent. They share a campaign budget and the same conversion tracking, but everything else is separate: keywords, ad copy, and landing page. This is the right way to build it. Mixing all three into one ad group would mean Google picks which keywords to spend on, and the ad copy would have to cover all three pain points at once. Splitting them means the ads are relevant, the landing pages match, and Quality Score stays high.

03 Why the prospecting looks good

The traffic this campaign will attract is genuinely qualified. Here is why I think that.

Every keyword in Campaign 2 contains a specific AWS intent: cost, migration, or security. There is no generic cloud traffic in here. If someone's Googling "aws cost audit" they are already an AWS customer with a real spend problem, not someone evaluating cloud providers for the first time. That narrows the audience in the right direction.

Phrase and Exact match only means we only show when the search contains our phrase, not when Google guesses it might be related. The A$18 bid cap keeps us competitive on these high-intent queries without overpaying. For context, AWS services keywords in Australia typically cost A$5 to A$15 per click. At A$18 we are well covered for almost every relevant auction.

The free assessment offer is the right hook at this stage of the funnel. Someone pricing up an AWS migration doesn't want to talk to sales; they want actual numbers. A free assessment from a senior AWS engineer is a direct answer to what they're searching for. Same logic applies to cost optimisation and security. We're not asking them to buy anything. We're offering to do the work that justifies the conversation.

Campaign #2 vs Campaign #1

Campaign 1 catches people who already know they want a managed services partner and are comparing options. Campaign 2 goes earlier: it catches people who have a specific pain point and haven't necessarily started thinking about a partner yet. The two campaigns complement each other. Campaign 1 converts faster; Campaign 2 builds the pipeline at a lower budget and feeds into that same funnel once the assessment is done.

06 Conversion tracking

Why LP Form Submit, not Consultation Request

Campaign 1 uses Consultation Request - Form Submit as its conversion signal. That tag fires specifically on the /active/ landing page form. Campaign 2 uses LP Form Submit, which fires on any /thank-you page and covers all three assessment landing pages.

Keeping these separate matters. If both campaigns attributed to the same conversion action, the data would blend and we'd lose visibility into which campaign and which funnel is actually generating leads. With separate conversion goals, we can see exactly what Campaign 2 is contributing on its own.

07 What to expect
First few hours
Google reviews the ads. All three RSAs should clear review within 1 to 2 hours and start serving on relevant searches.
First 24 to 48 hours
First impressions and clicks across the three ad groups. At A$15/day expect around 1 to 3 clicks per day initially while Google builds impression share. CPC should stay well under the A$18 cap on most queries.
First week
Check the Search Terms report. You'll see exactly what queries triggered the ads. Add any irrelevant terms as negatives early. This is the most valuable week for learning what the audience actually searches for.
Weeks 2 to 4
Watch which ad group is spending and converting. Cost Optimisation tends to be the highest volume intent. Migration and Security may have lower click volume but higher deal value. First conversions expected by end of week 2 if the landing pages are converting cleanly.
After 30 conversions
Move from Maximise Clicks to Maximise Conversions or Target CPA. Smart Bidding needs roughly 30 conversions in a 30 day window before it can optimise effectively. At A$15/day this might take 4 to 8 weeks depending on volume.
Budget note for early days

A$15/day across three ad groups means roughly A$5/day per funnel in the first few weeks. Google will spread this based on auction availability. If one ad group dominates spend early, that just means it has more matching search volume. Don't reallocate budget too quickly; let the data accumulate for at least 2 weeks before making changes.

08 Open follow-ups
Pull Search Terms report at day 7 Antonio · Week 1

After a week live, pull the Search Terms report and add any junk queries as negatives. This is the single most impactful thing to do in the first two weeks. The starter negative list covers the obvious waste terms, but real query data will surface things we couldn't have anticipated.

Check which ad group spends first Week 1 to 2

Watch the ad group breakdown in the campaign view. If Cost Optimisation is taking most of the budget because it has higher search volume, that's expected. If Migration or Security aren't spending at all after 10 days, the bid cap may need adjusting for those specific ad groups.

Verify LP Form Submit fires on all three landing pages First form submission

When the first real lead comes in, check that the LP Form Submit conversion is recording against Campaign 2, not Campaign 1. It should, because the conversion goal override is set at campaign level, but worth confirming on the first one.

Increase budget after first conversions After proof of conversion

Once we see conversions coming through, there is a case for lifting the daily budget from A$15 to A$25 or A$30 on the ad group that's converting. This is a short conversation once the data supports it.

Switch to Maximise Conversions after 30 conversions ~4 to 8 weeks out

Maximise Clicks is the right starting strategy. Once we accumulate 30 conversions in a rolling 30 day window, switch to Maximise Conversions or set a Target CPA. Smart Bidding at that stage will outperform manual bidding significantly.
