Amazon Redshift RG Instances: Graviton Performance Uplift

Post 01 of 03
Redshift just got meaningfully faster and cheaper at the same time
AWS To Review
Amazon Redshift · RG Instances
Generally Available 2.4x Faster 30% Lower Cost Graviton
Amazon Redshift has launched RG instances, a new generation of provisioned cluster nodes powered by AWS Graviton processors. The headline numbers are worth paying attention to: up to 2.4x faster than the previous RA3 instances, at 30% lower price per vCPU. The performance gain comes from the Graviton architecture combined with Redshift's custom-built vectorised data lake query engine. That engine runs directly on open table formats including Apache Iceberg and Parquet, which matters if your architecture spans a data warehouse and a data lake. Query processing happens closer to the data rather than going back and forth between systems. The 30% reduction in price per vCPU is a real number and the performance uplift compounds it. If your Redshift cluster is running RA3 instances, the economic case for migrating is straightforward. The integrated data lake query engine is the other thing worth evaluating. Teams running hybrid architectures, where some data sits in S3 and some in Redshift, have historically had to manage that boundary carefully. RG instances with native Iceberg support reduce that friction without requiring architectural changes. If you are running provisioned Redshift today, this is a migration worth scoping. The cost and performance improvement combined with open format support makes the upgrade path fairly clear. #AWS #AmazonRedshift #DataWarehouse #Graviton #CloudCost

Amazon Bedrock: Advanced Prompt Optimization Tool

Post 02 of 03
Prompt optimisation in Bedrock now has a dedicated tool
AWS To Review
Amazon Bedrock · Advanced Prompt Optimization
Generally Available Model Migration 5 Models Parallel Eval Feedback Loop
Amazon Bedrock has introduced Advanced Prompt Optimization, a tool that addresses one of the more time-consuming parts of working with foundation models in production. The problem is well known to anyone who has shipped AI workloads at scale. Optimising prompts is slow and iterative. When you want to move to a newer model, prompts that performed well on the previous model rarely transfer cleanly. You have to rebuild, test, and validate from scratch, with no systematic way to prevent regressions as you go. Advanced Prompt Optimization gives you a structured workflow for this. You can compare optimised outputs across up to five models simultaneously, which makes model selection and migration decisions considerably faster. The built-in evaluation feedback loop means you are testing against a defined baseline rather than manually assessing outputs. The migration use case is the more important one for teams with existing Bedrock workloads. If you have prompts tuned for an older model, the tool gives you a repeatable path for porting them to newer models without losing the performance characteristics you have already validated. For teams building at scale on Bedrock, this reduces the maintenance overhead that accumulates as the model landscape evolves. That is a meaningful efficiency gain when you have dozens of prompts running in production. #AWS #AmazonBedrock #GenerativeAI #PromptEngineering #AIEngineering

AWS WAF: AI Traffic Analysis Dashboards

Post 03 of 03
AWS WAF now shows you exactly what your AI traffic looks like
AWS To Review
AWS WAF · AI Traffic Analysis
AI Bot Visibility Traffic Intelligence Policy Control
AWS has added AI Traffic Analysis dashboards to AWS WAF, and for teams running web-facing applications, this is worth reviewing now. The context matters. AI agents and automated programmatic traffic now account for a meaningful and growing share of web requests hitting most applications. The challenge is that most WAF configurations were designed around human traffic patterns. Bot traffic has always been a concern, but the nature of automated access is changing: AI agents are more sophisticated, harder to fingerprint, and increasingly legitimate in some contexts while problematic in others. The new dashboards give you visibility into AI bot and agent activity specifically, rather than lumping it into the generic bot traffic category. You can see what is hitting your application, where it is coming from, and whether it is behaving in ways that warrant a policy response. The operational value is in separating signal from noise. Security teams spending time investigating traffic anomalies without this kind of categorisation are working harder than they need to. Having AI-specific visibility in WAF means you can make policy decisions based on actual traffic composition rather than broad rules that catch everything and nothing. If your application is publicly accessible and your WAF policies have not been reviewed recently, AI traffic patterns are a legitimate reason to revisit them. #AWS #CloudSecurity #AWSWAF #AISecurity #WebSecurity
